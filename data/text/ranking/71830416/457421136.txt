I. 
Overview

7. 
OPI is a real estate investment trust, or REIT, formed in 2009 under Maryland law.

The Company owns and leases high-quality office and mixed-use properties in select, growth-

oriented U.S. markets. The Company’s property portfolio consists of 124 wholly owned properties

located in 29 states and the District of Columbia, containing approximately 17.2 million rentable

square feet.3 As of the Petition Date, the Debtors wholly own 117 properties containing

approximately 15.9 million rentable square feet. The U.S. government is the Company’s largest

tenant, representing approximately 17.1% of the Company’s annualized rental income. The

Company is managed by The RMR Group LLC (“RMR” or the “Manager”) pursuant to the

Company’s management agreements with RMR. The Company has no employees and the

personnel and various services the Company requires to operate its properties are provided by

RMR pursuant to those management agreements. RMR is an alternative asset management

company focused on commercial real estate and related businesses.

estate financing market, reduced government spending, and changing government budget

II. 
Operations

19. 
OPI is a REIT focused on owning and leasing high-quality office and mixed-use

office properties in select, growth-oriented markets throughout the United States. The Company

has a diversified revenue base across geographies with ownership in central business district, urban

infill, and suburban locations.

A. 
Corporate History

20. 
OPI was formed in 2009 under Maryland law under the name Government

Properties Income Trust. OPI was initially a wholly owned subsidiary of HRPT Properties Trust

(subsequently known as CommonWealth REIT) (“HRPT”)4. With 29 majority-government-leased

properties at its inception, OPI, initially a subsidiary of HRPT, completed its initial public offering

in June 2009, becoming a separate, publicly owned company. HRPT (then known as

CommonWealth REIT) was the Company’s largest shareholder until March 2013, when

CommonWealth REIT sold all of the OPI common shares it held in a public offering.

21. 
Historically, the Company has grown its property portfolio through strategic

acquisitions. Significantly, in July 2014, the Company acquired approximately 35.9% of the then-

outstanding common shares of Select Income REIT (“SIR”)5 from HRPT. In October 2017, OPI

acquired First Potomac Realty Trust (“FPO”) by merger in an approximately $1.4 billion

transaction, which included the repayment of approximately $483 million of FPO corporate debt

and assumption of $167.5 million of mortgage debt in connection. In connection with the FPO

transaction, the Company acquired 72 properties with approximately 6.5 million square feet, and

FPO’s 50% and 51% interests in two joint ventures that owned three properties. Subsequently, in

C. 
Current Operations

1. 
Business Strategy

28. 
The Company operates in one business segment: ownership of real estate

properties. The Company’s business strategy has centered on increasing rents and cash flows from

its existing portfolio by (a) entering in to new or renewal leases, (b) repositioning and

redevelopment activities, and (c) growing through its acquisition, disposition, and financing

policies comprising its investment strategy. The Company’s primary investment objectives include

increasing operational cash flows from stable and diverse sources and acquiring properties that

enhance the Company’s overall portfolio composition and produce greater returns than the

properties or portfolios it sells as part of its disposition strategy.

29. 
Although the Company’s business plan does not currently contemplate the

acquisition of additional properties, the Company’s investment focus is on office properties

primarily in markets that have strong economic fundamentals to support growth, including

(a) properties primarily leased to single tenants, particularly those that are strategic to the tenant

(e.g., built-to suit properties, corporate headquarters, and buildings where tenants have invested

meaningful capital) and with a minimum remaining lease term of seven years; (b) properties leased

to government tenants; and (c) first-generation buildings where there is a reasonably high

probability of renewing the tenant in place and where ongoing capital needs are expected to be

modest. The Company also expects to continue to maintain its capital recycling program and

A. 
External and Internal Challenges

76. 
Office Real Estate Sector. The Company’s principal sources of funds for meeting

operating and capital expenses, paying debt service obligations, and making required distributions

to its shareholders are the operating cash flows the Company generates from its properties, net

proceeds from property sales, and availability and proceeds from the Company’s funded debt

obligations. In recent years, the office industry has been adversely affected by shifts in office space

utilization, including increased remote work arrangements and tenants consolidating their real

estate footprint, as well as ongoing market and economic conditions, including a challenging real

estate financing market, reduced government spending, and changing budget priorities. As a result,

the demand for office space continues to face headwinds, and declining rents and increasing costs

to re-lease space when new tenants can be identified continue to impact the commercial real estate

market, including for both private sector and government tenants and in markets where the

Company has a high concentration of properties, such as Washington D.C.

77. 
Moreover, the Company’s current portfolio has not been well positioned to address

these market factors. Older, single-tenant suburban office properties with few amenities are

challenging to lease in today’s market environment. In addition, leasing conditions remain

challenging as competition among landlords continues to put further pressure on rents.

78. 
Inflation, Interest Rates, Governmental Pressures. Higher interest rates, lack of

capital and financing for office properties, inflationary pressures, government policies (including

the potential reduction of U.S. federal office leases), geopolitical hostilities and tensions, and

concerns that the U.S. economy may enter an economic recession have caused disruptions in the

financial markets. These factors have affected the Company’s and its tenants’ financial condition

and, in turn, the ability or willingness of the Company’s tenants to renew their leases. Higher

interest rates have also led to high interest rates under the Company’s recently issued debt

instruments, which have significantly increased the Company’s borrowing costs.

interest rates and market sentiment towards the office sector have restricted the Company’s access

to, and increased its cost of, capital, have caused the values of the Company’s properties and its

operations, financial position and cash flows. Rental incomes have declined at least 3.5% year over
