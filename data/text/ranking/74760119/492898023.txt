Introduction

4.
The Debtors own and operate the world’s only global golf league (the “League”)

featuring 13 teams, a 14-tournament schedule, and many of the world’s best golfers.  The League

was designed to expand the sport of golf on a global level and offer a new and exciting alternative

to the PGA Tour and DP World Tour—established leagues which have historically dominated the

market.  To meet this goal, the League has engaged in constant innovation to set a new standard

in the sport and redefine the fan experience through the lens of music, culture, and entertainment,

while growing the game of golf for a new era of players and fans around the world.

5.
In that vein, LIV Golf sought to change the entire approach to how the game was

played by golfers and viewed by fans.  Gameplay was accelerated through shotgun starts which

reduced the average pace of play from 10-12 hours for a traditional golf tournament to only 4.5

hours for a LIV Golf tournament.  In addition, LIV introduced a team concept to golf, whereby

players compete both on an individual and team basis.  This model offered greater excitement to

fans, a more condensed broadcast experience as well as new sponsorship/branding opportunities.

Finally, LIV Golf added a festival-style atmosphere to their tournaments, with extensive fan

entertainment options, including celebrity appearances and concerts.  This allowed the League to

attract a younger, more diverse audience compared to traditional golf in line with its mission of

growing the game of golf globally.

6.
Acquiring top-tier talent was an important aspect to developing a premier golf

league.  To effectively establish itself as a serious contender, LIV Golf needed to find a way to

recruit and retain high-performing golfers with strong fan recognition—a high hurdle in the face

of unrelenting anticompetitive opposition to LIV Golf by the established leagues.  Despite these

Top Talent.

20.
For the established golf tours, the bulk (or all) of the golfers’ compensation is based

on individual performance at tournaments in the form of individual prize purses.  In addition, other

golf tours use a “cut” model (which is not utilized by LIV Golf) whereby golfers whose total

to the final two days and receive no prize money—while still incurring out-of-pocket costs

required to compete.  By contrast, LIV Golf utilizes a more stabilized compensation model to

obtain talent with some guaranteed money, which makes the tournament more attractive to

sponsors and fans.  Specifically, LIV Golf compensates its players with a mix of (i) guaranteed up

front “commitment” payments and “annual” payments, (ii) team equity for certain players2, and

(iii) performance-based individual and team-based prize purses.  In exchange, players granted

sponsorship rights to the League and/or their teams, subject to the rights retained under their

respective contracts, and some players also gave up their name, image and likeness rights.
